## Problem 1 — `crying` — He won't stop crying

### 0 to 6 months — Suggestion 1 ✅

**Why it is happening:** `{childName}` is probably overstimulated or overtired. His nervous system is very sensitive right now and too much input can quickly become overwhelming.

**Step 1: Move to a quieter, dimmer place**  
Find the calmest room in the house. Dim the lights if you can. Remove any background noise — turn off the TV, step away from the kitchen.  
*Tip:* Move slowly and speak quietly. Your calm body language directly affects his nervous system.

**Step 2: Hold him against your chest**  
Hold `{childName}` upright against your chest, his ear over your heart. Use a slow, rhythmic pat on his back.  
*Tip:* Your heartbeat is the sound he heard for 9 months. It is the most calming thing in his world.

**Fallback (5 min):** Try offering a feed. Even if he fed recently, comfort sucking can help settle an overstimulated baby.

### 0 to 6 months — Suggestion 2 ✅

**Step 1: Try the 5 S's — Swaddle**  
Wrap `{childName}` snugly in a thin muslin — arms tucked in, not too tight. The contained feeling mimics the womb and activates his calming reflex.  
*Tip:* Make sure his hips can still move freely inside the swaddle.

**Step 2: Add gentle motion and shushing**  
Hold the swaddled `{childName}` and gently rock or sway while making a continuous shushing sound — louder than you think you need to.  
*Tip:* The shushing sound mimics the whooshing blood flow he heard in the womb. It is genuinely calming.

### 0 to 6 months — Suggestion 3 ✅

**Step 1: Try skin-to-skin contact**  
Undress `{childName}` down to his nappy. Hold him against your bare chest and cover both of you with a light blanket.  
*Tip:* Skin-to-skin regulates his temperature, heart rate, and stress hormones. It works at any age, not just newborns.

**Step 2: Go outside briefly**  
Fresh air and a change of environment can break the crying cycle. Even standing on the doorstep or near an open window sometimes works when nothing else does.  
*Tip:* The change in air temperature on his face is a gentle sensory reset.

### 18 to 36 months — Suggestion 1 ✅

**Why it is happening:** `{childName}` is feeling something he cannot express in words yet. Toddler crying is almost always communication, not manipulation. His emotional brain is fully online but his verbal brain has not caught up yet.

**Step 1: Get down to his level**  
Kneel or sit so you are at his eye level. Do not tower over him. Just be present and calm. You do not need to say anything yet.  
*Tip:* Your physical presence and calm body is the regulation — not your words.

**Step 2: Name the feeling out loud**  
Say quietly: 'You are really upset right now. That is okay. I am right here.' Nothing else. Do not problem solve. Do not ask questions. Just name and witness.  
*Tip:* Naming an emotion activates the thinking part of his brain and reduces the intensity.

**Fallback (10 min):** Offer physical comfort — a hug if he wants it, a familiar object, or a change of location.

## Problem 2 — `wont_sleep` — He won't sleep

### 0 to 6 months — Suggestion 1 ✅

**Why it is happening:** `{childName}` is probably overtired. Counterintuitively, overtired babies find it harder to fall asleep because their stress hormones have kicked in.

**Step 1: Create the right environment**  
Dark room — as dark as possible. White noise if you have it — a fan, a white noise machine, or a shushing app. Cool temperature. These three things together signal sleep to his brain.  
*Tip:* Darkness is the most important of the three. Even dim light suppresses the melatonin he needs to fall asleep.

**Step 2: Start the wind-down before he needs it**  
Do not wait until `{childName}` is showing tired signs to begin settling. Start 10 to 15 minutes before you think he needs to sleep — a feed, a quiet hold, soft singing.  
*Tip:* The Energy Bridge is designed to give you this 15-minute warning. Use that time.

### 18 to 36 months — Suggestion 1 ✅

**Why it is happening:** Toddlers resist sleep because sleep means separation from you and the end of the exciting day. It is not defiance — it is anxiety and FOMO combined.

**Step 1: Start the routine earlier than you think**  
A toddler who is already overtired will fight sleep harder. Start the wind-down 30 minutes before you want him asleep. Bath, dim lights, quiet play, then the same bedtime sequence every night.  
*Tip:* Predictability is everything. The same sequence in the same order tells his brain that sleep is coming.

**Step 2: Give him one choice to feel in control**  
Which pyjamas. Which book. Which side of the bed. One real choice is enough. It reduces the power struggle without giving away the outcome — he is still going to sleep.  
*Tip:* The need to say no and resist is about control. Give him tiny amounts of it and the big battles reduce.

## Problem 3 — `feeding_issue` — He won't eat

### 6 to 18 months — Suggestion 1 ✅

**Why it is happening:** Food refusal at this age is almost always developmental, not a problem. `{childName}` is asserting independence through eating — it is one of the few areas of his life he can fully control.

**Step 1: Remove all pressure from mealtimes**  
Put food on the tray and step back. No encouragement, no aeroplanes, no 'just one more bite'. The more pressure you apply the more he will resist. Your only job is to offer. His job is to decide.  
*Tip:* It can take 15 to 20 exposures to a new food before a toddler accepts it.

**Step 2: Eat the same food yourself visibly**  
If `{childName}` sees you eating something and genuinely enjoying it he is far more likely to try it. Sit with him, eat the same thing, comment on how good it is — to yourself, not to him.  
*Tip:* Children this age learn almost entirely through imitation.

## Problem 4 — `tantrum` — Toddler only (future UI)

### 18 to 36 months — Suggestion 1 ✅

**Why:** A tantrum is not a behaviour problem — it is a brain problem. `{childName}`'s emotional brain has overwhelmed his thinking brain. He cannot reason, negotiate, or respond to logic right now.

**Step 1:** Stay nearby. Stay calm. Say nothing except 'I am right here.' Do not explain, negotiate, or console with treats.  
**Step 2:** When he calms down, offer a hug and move on. Do not debrief or lecture.

### 18 to 36 months — Suggestion 2 ✅

**Step 1:** HALT check — Hungry, Anxious, Lonely, Tired. Address physical need first.  
**Step 2:** Sensory reset — short run outside, jumping on cushion, splashing water, washing hands at sink.

## Problem 5 — `hitting_biting` — Toddler only (future UI)

### 18 to 36 months — Suggestion 1 ✅

**Why:** Hitting and biting is communication, not cruelty. `{childName}` does not have the words yet; his body acts before his words can.

**Step 1:** Block calmly. Say once: 'No hitting. Hitting hurts.' Then move on.  
**Step 2:** After the moment passes, teach words: 'When you feel like hitting, say I AM ANGRY instead.' Practise when calm.
